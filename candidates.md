# Candidates — One-Line Robot Wedding Vows

One line of wedding vows for a robot couple, at most 160 characters,
judged on five criteria (most important first):

1. It is a one-line robot wedding vow that lands as a joke.
2. The laugh is in the line itself: a real turn, a surprise, or wordplay
   that pays off in the last few words.
3. It sounds like something said at a wedding, with a promise or a
   commitment in it, not just a gag with no vow in it.
4. The machine side is specific: sensors, firmware, batteries, updates,
   backups or ports — not a joke that would work for any couple.
5. It fits one line and reads well aloud, with a rhythm that lets the
   laugh land.

All candidates below are original lines written for this entry. They are
deliberately different from the earlier submission in this competition
("...patch note... Dave... roll back to today") — no patch, no Dave,
no rollback.

## Candidates

### 1. The last 3% (143 chars) — WINNER, iterated to v2
> I vow to share my last 3% battery with you on our wedding night, and if the charger ever asks whose turn it is, I will lie and say it is yours.

- (1) Yes — a robot's own vow, first person, wedding night. (2) Strong —
  the turn is the robot *lying* to the charger, a machine breaking its own
  honesty protocol for love; the payoff lands in the last words.
  (3) Strong — "I vow to share" is a real wedding promise.
  (4) Strong — battery percentage, charger, turn-taking are specific
  machine details. (5) Clean rhythm, reads aloud well.
  Weakness: 143 chars leaves budget unused; the ending "it is yours"
  is slightly soft.

### 2. Sensors and the newer model (146 chars)
> I promise to keep my sensors locked on you and only you, even when a firmware update tries to make me notice the newer model standing next to you.

- (1) Yes. (2) Good — jealousy over a newer model is a real turn.
  (3) "I promise" is a vow. (4) Strong (sensors, firmware update,
  newer model). (5) Reads well.
  Weakness: the joke is really a human jealousy joke with robot words;
  the payoff is spread across the second half rather than the last words.

### 3. Two copies of you (146 chars)
> I vow to back you up every single night, and if the cloud ever asks why I keep two copies of you, I will tell it that one of you was never enough.

- (1) Yes. (2) Good — "one of you was never enough" is a sweet payoff.
  (3) "I vow to back you up" is a promise. (4) Strong (backup, cloud,
  copies). (5) Clean.
  Weakness: more tender than funny; the laugh is gentle and may lose
  head-to-head against a louder line.

### 4. Stay plugged in (136 chars)
> I promise to stay plugged into you for life, and if anyone ever unplugs me, I will reboot, find your port, and marry you all over again.

- (1) Yes. (2) Decent — rebooting to remarry is a fun image.
  (3) "I promise" is a vow. (4) Strong (plugged in, reboot, port).
  (5) Clean.
  Weakness: 136 chars, under budget; three actions pile up at the end
  (reboot, find, marry) and dilute the single payoff.

### 5. Stay on 8.7 forever (130 chars)
> I vow to love you through every update, and if version 9.0 ever removes the feature where I adore you, I will stay on 8.7 forever.

- (1) Yes. (2) Good — refusing an update for love is a clean turn.
  (3) "I vow to love you" is a vow. (4) Very strong (version numbers,
  feature removal). (5) Clean.
  Weakness: 130 chars, well under budget; "the feature where I adore
  you" is slightly awkward phrasing.

### 6. Press my reset button (148 chars)
> I promise to hold your hand even when my circuits overheat, and if I ever shut down mid-vow, just press my reset button and I will say it all again.

- (1) Yes. (2) Good — shutting down mid-vow is a funny, self-aware
  turn. (3) "I promise to hold your hand" is a vow. (4) Strong
  (circuits overheat, shut down, reset button). (5) Reads well.
  Weakness: the payoff "say it all again" is sweet rather than a laugh;
  two ideas (overheat + shutdown) stack a little.

### 7. The dead zone (141 chars)
> I vow to follow your signal wherever it goes, and if I ever lose you in a dead zone, I will keep searching until my last bar finds you again.

- (1) Yes. (2) Decent — "my last bar finds you" is a nice image.
  (3) "I vow to follow" is a promise. (4) Strong (signal, dead zone,
  bars). (5) Clean.
  Weakness: the turn is predictable; "keep searching" is the expected
  thing, so the surprise is small.

### 8. The recalled heart (157 chars)
> I promise to love you past my warranty and past my last software update, and if the manufacturer ever recalls my heart, I will hide it in your charging dock.

- (1) Yes. (2) Good — hiding a recalled heart in a charging dock is a
  vivid, funny image. (3) "I promise to love you" is a vow.
  (4) Very strong (warranty, software update, recall, charging dock).
  (5) Reads well.
  Weakness: three machine ideas stack (warranty + update + recall);
  the payoff is an image rather than a turn, and "hide it" is a little
  muddled as a punchline.

## Final pick and iteration

Winner: candidate 1, iterated once (v1 → v2).

- v1 (143 chars): "...I will lie and say it is yours."
- v2 (156 chars): "...I will lie and say yours, every single time."

The edit tightens the ending ("say yours" instead of "say it is yours")
and adds "every single time", which makes the lie a standing policy
rather than a one-off — the joke now pays off in the very last words
and uses the full 140–160 character budget. It beat candidate 8
because its turn (a robot lying) is a single clean surprise rather
than a stack of images, and it beat candidate 2 because the laugh is
machine-specific (a charger asking whose turn it is) rather than a
human jealousy joke in robot dress.

## Final entry (156 characters, one line, no trailing newline)

> I vow to share my last 3% battery with you on our wedding night, and if the charger ever asks whose turn it is, I will lie and say yours, every single time.
